## Notes

- If your product vision is the long-term outcome you hope to deliver with your product, then your product strategy defines how you’ll get there.
- Your strategy is also the link between your roadmap and your product vision—the strategy turns the vision into actions you can take to achieve those long-term goals.

**Market and Needs:** Define target audience and solve the problems in a way that haven’t been done before.

**Key features and differentiates:** Identify competitive advantages and value delivery

Ask following questions to yourself and your team to make sure the foundation:
- What are the emotional reactions you’re hoping to get from your users every time you release a feature?
- What unique purpose will your product fulfill in the market?
- What are the valuable aspects you need to build into your product?
- What resources (time, effort, money) does the company have to achieve that value?
- What are the limitations and possible snags your product might face in the market?

**[Roman Pichler’s product strategy canvas](https://www.romanpichler.com/blog/the-product-canvas/?=undefined)** is a neat tool for developing a solid definition of who your target audience is, what their problems are, and how you can turn those problems into actionable tasks for the team to work on.

**[Melissa Perri’s version](https://melissaperri.com/blog/2016/07/14/what-is-good-product-strategy?=undefined)** of the product canvas is slightly different. It’s a more informal version of the in-depth version developed by Roman Pichler.
